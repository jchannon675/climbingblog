## About Me

I was born in 1998 in Monterey, California. After moving around a bit during the early years of life, we ultimately landed in the Santa Ynez Valley by the time I was due to start elementary school. After graduating high school, I moved from Santa Ynez to nearby Santa Barbara to attend SBCC. I worked food service jobs and drifted between majors for a bit, completing 2 years of an English major before a brief stint of electrical engineering, where my Intro to Coding class introduced me to coding. It seemed to click for me, and preferring Python to assembly, I switched majors once again to computer science. I transferred to CSU Channel Islands in 2022 and graduated in 2024. I started an internship at a company in Goleta during my time in school, and started full-time as an analyst in late 2023 after finishing classes. This is where I’m still working today, doing a wide variety of software-related tasks.

In late 2017 I met my partner, a lovely lady who I’d gone to high school and worked in a burger joint with, but never had the nerves or excuse to talk to until she came in and ordered a smoothie from me while I was working at Blenders in the Grass. She’ll be upset if I don’t mention that I gave her the wrong smoothie. In 2019 I adopted a 2-year-old Belgian Malinois from the local shelter, having no idea what I was getting myself into. A few years later we added a scrappy little terrier mix to the entourage, finding out with a DNA test that she is mostly Chihuahua and pit bull with a conglomeration of other random breeds. Both the dogs have accompanied both Jiv and I on many adventures, from hiking in Santa Barbara rainstorms to swimming in alpine lakes in the Eastern Sierra and so much more. These days Daxter, the Malinois, is slowing down and getting some gray hairs. Sahara, the Chihuahua, is a bit more independent and sassy. Our little family is getting a little older, but just as crazy and chaotic in our apartment as ever.

In May of 2024 Daxter and I went camping with some friends outside Lone Pine, at the base of the Eastern Sierra. Sitting alone with Dax late at night, I watched the moon come down over the needles of Mount Whitney and wondered how many people were camped in the valleys between the granite peaks and ridges. After getting back I was fascinated by the idea of the summit and started researching about the different ways to the top. The Mountaineer’s Route piqued my interest. A big hiking day with some scrambling sounded like some of my favorite days in the Santa Barbara mountains. I started to learn about how to keep oneself safe with a rope, which led to learning about rock climbing. Somewhere along this path, in September of 2024, I realized I could buy a rope and a TRS setup and go to San Ysidro trail, where I had seen bolts and anchors on a massive rock slab. So I did just that, hiked up the walk-off, and fixed my new rope to a couple of beefy bolts at the top of the slab. Shaking and terrified, I leaned backwards and rappelled into a new world.

Fast forward two years and I have had countless amazing experiences and made just as many new friends from this new world of climbing. I climbed up Mount Whitney and came down my coveted Mountaineer’s Route, and even ran up the neighboring Mount Russell in a day via the Mithril Dihedral route. I’ve been spending this summer fully diving into alpine climbing in the Sierra, and now that summer is ending I have so many goals for when the snow thaws. I recently climbed the South Face of Washington Column in a day, and am eager to dive further into the world of big wall climbing. I’ve explored so many new places, and made lasting friendships scattered across California and Colorado, and lasting memories with them. Climbing has given me so much more than I ever could have foreseen when fixing that rope in San Ysidro.

## About This Site

I like to write, and I like to share photos of my adventures. Until now, that’s been relegated to Instagram posts and text messages. Since discovering stephabegg.com, and other similar climbing trip report blogs, I’ve been excited about the idea of creating one of my own. To have a collection of recollections, along with as many pictures and as long of a yap as I desire. To share those with whoever cares about the adventures, just friends and family. That is what this site is, and I plan to populate it with stories of adventure, life, and doggies.
